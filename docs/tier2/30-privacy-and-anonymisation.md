# 30 — Privacy and corpus anonymisation

*Tier 2: authored from `vrp/anonymise.py`, `vrp/bench/corpus.py`,
`tests/vrp/test_anonymisation.py`.*

## The requirement and the gap it closed

Benchmark corpora must be anonymised and coordinate-obfuscated before leaving
the production boundary.

Everything in `vrp/bench` today is *specified* rather than collected, so no
address has ever left — but the module is blunt that this is not a control:

> that is a property of where the fixtures came from, not a control. The day a
> corpus is derived from real deliveries there is nothing to stop it being
> written, and the way that failure surfaces is that it is already public.

So the gate exists **before** there is real data to run through it. A corpus
passes it to be written.

## Two halves, honestly weighted

**Stripping identifiers is the strong half.** An `order_id` joins straight back
to the production system. Without it, a record is "a weight at a place".

**Moving the coordinate is the weak half.** It defeats the doorstep; it does
not defeat a determined match against a known address list, and — this is the
part usually left unsaid — "no grid resolution rescues the isolated rural
delivery that is the only record in its cell".

The module measured this on the shipped corpus rather than asserting it:

| Grid | Effect |
|---|---|
| 500 m | 73% of stops collapse onto a shared point; **14% are still alone in their cell** |

Coarsening buys little anonymity and costs the benchmark its geometry. That
measurement is why the grid is 50 m rather than something that sounds safer.

## Why 50 m, derived rather than rounded up for comfort

The median nearest-neighbour distance over all 50,000 shipped stops is **66 m**,
and a 50 m grid displaces a point by at most **35 m**. So a stop never moves
past its nearest neighbour, and **the local ordering a solver exploits survives
the obfuscation** — which is the whole point of anonymising a *benchmark*
rather than merely a dataset.

`MEDIAN_SPACING_METRES` pins the measurement at 60 rather than 66 so a
regenerated corpus drifting a few metres denser does not fail the suite for a
reason that has nothing to do with privacy.

This is the general pattern worth copying: **derive the threshold from the
geometry**, because a literal constant stops separating the cases the moment
the data changes and says nothing about why it was chosen.

## Where the gate sits

The gate is on the **write** path of a corpus, not on a review checklist. A
corpus derived from real deliveries cannot be written without passing it.

Note the corpus itself is **regenerated by the gate's own pipeline** — patching
a committed corpus file by hand does not survive; the generator is the thing to
change.

## What this does not cover

The scope here is benchmark corpora leaving the production boundary. It is not
a data-protection posture for the running system, and the code does not claim
to be one. In particular:

- **Cache keys carry coordinates in plaintext.** `gateway/src/cache.rs` says so
  directly: the endpoint stays unhashed as a key prefix, so "keys are not safe
  to log wholesale". Anything that dumps Redis keys, or logs them at debug
  level, is handling location data.
- **Metric labels are deliberately bounded** to avoid the same leak: `/tile`
  collapses to its route pattern and upstream endpoints collapse to a
  five-value `service` label, "because the raw endpoint carries the request's
  coordinates" (doc 17).
- **Snapshots retain the real instance** — that is their purpose. Retention
  period, storage and access control are deployment decisions the code does not
  make (doc 29).
- **No retention policy, no PII classification, and no DSAR path** exist in the
  repository.
